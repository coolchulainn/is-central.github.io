## Intro

In IS5, all competetive strategies can be divided into two overarching styles based on the item they choose at the F1 encounter "Fated Encounter", namely either Lamp of Wishes and Talons of Hatred. These items are so strategy-defining that tournaments allow players to restart their run if they did not get the encounter, and it's also common for tournaments to demand that teams have an equal spread of Lamp and Talons players. Understanding Lamp and Talons is the first step to understanding all other aspects of IS5 strategy.

The core difference between the two can be boiled down to this: With Lamp, you have a very predictable power progression, but you're restricted in what operators you can draft if you want to stay on schedule for this progression. With Talons your progression is more unpredictable, but you have more freedom once you start snowballing and reap more rewards overall. 

## Lamp of Wishes

The main effect of Lamp of Wishes is that refreshing a node gives a high chance of turning it into a Wish Fulfilled, and Wish Fulfilled nodes have extra options that offer certain collectibles based on your current status, including Hand collectibles, King's collectibles, and ingot-based ASPD collectibles. This makes it very easy to guarantee getting a strong build by fulfilling these conditions, meaning that Lamp players will always have a clear gameplan on how to become strong enough to win. Simply lower your LP by entering nodes with Toil, refresh a node here and there, and pick up your full set of King's collectibles. 

A big restriction of Lamp comps is that you want to avoid drafting 6* operators who use more than one type of Hand collectible. This is because the Lamp will always prioritise offering you a Hand collectible for any 6* operators you recruited who don't have their matching Hand yet, which will delay you from getting to pick up the King's collectibles that actually give you your power spike. If the Hand in question also has a book with a matching name, Lamp will give you *that* one too before moving on to the King's set. This makes certain operator subclasses significantly more or less desirable for Lamp strategies depending on whether they have a matching Hand and whether it's worth building around that Hand. Drafting operators of multiple Hands and messing up your Lamp progression that way is called handmixing. 

Let's say I'm starting my run with Rosmontis. Rosmontis is a Flinger, meaning she uses the Hand of Rumble. If I enter a Wish Fulfilled with Rosmontis as my only 6* that corresponds to a Hand, it will give me a Hand of Rumble, and then the next Wish Fulfilled will start giving me King's collectibles if my LP is at 1. However, if I recruited Archetto between the first and second Wish Fulfilled nodes, the second Wish Fulfilled will instead give me the Hand of Fireworks for Marksman Snipers, and only give me the King's collectibles starting from the third Wish Fulfilled. If I had picked up Goldenglow instead of Archetto, my Wish Fulfilled nodes would give me Hand of Undulation second, then Broken Wand - Undulation third, and only *then* the King's collectibles. I might be at floor 4 by then, and struggling to clear stages when I don't have even a single King's buff. If I'd picked up old school W though, my second Wish Fulfilled would give me King's New Lance right on schedule, because W and Rosmontis share a Hand.

- Note that only 6* operators will prompt a Hand from Lamp. You can recruit 5* of any subclasses you like without mixing up your Hands.

Lamp also makes it easy to guarantee an obscene amount of ASPD buffs through Coin-Operated Toy, Golden Chalice, and the ingots gained from robbing the shop. This means operators that scale well with ASPD have significantly more value in Lamp. For example, Degenbrecher S3 is a fixed number of slashes over a fixed amount of time, so while buffing her ATK will increase her skill damage, buffing her ASPD has no effect on her S3 whatsoever. Hoederer meanwhile restores a huge chunk of his maximum HP on his offensive recovery S1 and has a chance to stun enemies with every attack on his mode-switch S2, so with extremely high ASPD he is constantly healing himself or stunning enemies, boosting not only his damage but also his survivability.

The rate at which you get your Lamp collectibles will depend on how many Plans you have available and what nodes you come across, as well as some luck in actually refreshing into a Wish Fulfilled. On higher difficulties with heavier toil, your opportunities to safely lower your LP will also become a factor. Ideally you don't refresh any combat nodes, since skipping those means skipping valuable command EXP and voucher drops as well, but there are situations where you simply need that King's collectible now if you want to survive. F4 guarantees a number of Wish Fulfilled nodes on the fifth collumn of the floor, usually with vertical paths between at least some of them, and Bizarre Fragments entered the normal way also always feature a Wish Fulfilled as their second node. These guaranteed nodes are good to keep in mind when planning your Lamp progression, since they can save you from having to spend Plans on refreshes. 

## Talons of Hatred

Talons increases the chance of a node refreshing into an Emergency operation and buffs both your and enemy units' ATK for an obvious risk-reward gamble. Winning runs with Talons is harder and more inconsistent than Lamp, but that same difficulty can make winning with Talons all the more satisfying. Collection squad and Ingots squad are common with Talons, since both are suited for the Talons gameplan of gathering as many different collectibles as possible. 

Since no specific collectibles are guaranteed like with Lamp, the specific form your build will take is less certain, and the baseline performance of your operators is more important. The high ASPD from Golden Chalice isn't guaranteed when playing Talons, so operators like Hoederer who rely on that high ASPD for their strong performance in Lamp have less value in Talons. However, you don't need to worry about handmixing when playing Talons, so you can instead use operators with overlapping or underperforming Hands that would be unviable in Lamp strategies such as Logos. 

The basic strategy of Talons is straightforward, just refresh as many nodes as you can into Emergency operations and reap those increased rewards. Prioritise refreshing non-combat nodes insofar getting the ending(s) you want allows, but if you have Plans to spare you can take a gamble on turning regular combat nodes into Emergencies too. Wish Fulfilled is a refresh target too, because with Talons the choice is between one random collectible from Wish Fulfilled or multiple random drops from beating the Emergency.
